## Who is this itinerary for?

A three-night, four-day Zuluk itinerary suits travellers who can reach NJP or Bagdogra early and accept several hours of mountain driving. A late arrival can make the plan unrealistic, so the first-day cutoff should appear in the quotation.

## Day 1: NJP or Bagdogra to Aritar or Mankhim

Drive to the first homestay and keep the afternoon flexible. The exact village can change according to accommodation, permit processing and route direction.

## Day 2: Aritar to Zuluk

Continue through the permitted route toward Zuluk, with stops selected according to daylight and road conditions. Expect basic, locally operated accommodation rather than city-hotel facilities.

## Day 3: Zuluk circuit and descent

Start early for the hairpin bends, Thambi View Point, Nathang Valley, Kupup Lake and other permitted points. Access depends on weather, snow, route permission and defence-area restrictions. Descend to a suitable overnight point such as Rishikhola when the operating plan allows.

## Day 4: Return to NJP or Bagdogra

Leave after breakfast and maintain a generous buffer before onward travel.

## Transparent price basis

A genuine starting price must name the minimum group size, vehicle-sharing basis, homestay standard, meals and pickup point. Permit processing may be included, but government restrictions cannot be overridden. Entry fees and traveller-requested route additions should be itemized.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the full Silk Route always open?

No. Access is condition-dependent and can change at short notice.

### Are luxury hotels available in Zuluk?

Accommodation is usually simple homestay-style lodging. Expectations should be discussed before booking.

### Is this suitable for a two-night trip?

A two-night plan is possible only with limited coverage and suitable arrival timing. Three nights is generally more balanced.
